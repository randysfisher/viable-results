What the page is doing well

Signals worth protecting through any rewrite.

The page already matches the compliance mandate

33% of converting compliance officers pointed to the page tying the suite directly to consent, preferences, opt-ins and opt-outs, and rights management. Another 28% cited the concrete capability bullets, such as cookie and tracker scanning and DSAR workflows. The substance is right. What is missing is the path to action.

Third-party proof is converting across every audience

The G2 and Forrester TEI evidence, at 126% ROI and $2.08M in benefit, was named as a conversion driver by 17% of compliance officers, 22% of IT security, and 16% of SME DPOs. This is the one element that works on all three audiences at once, which makes it the wrong thing to move or dilute.

What is holding it back

Four issues account for most of the simulated non-conversions, and they share a root: the page explains the category well and the decision badly.

The demo request competes with your own links

26% of compliance officers and 26% of IT security visitors explicitly did not convert because "Request a Demo" sits alongside many "Learn more" links across the app sections. At the exact moment the page should concentrate intent into one action, it offers a dozen ways to keep reading instead.

The top promise reads marketing-led, not role-led

23% of compliance officers and 22% of IT security visitors rejected the page over the framing. "Maximize customer trust" and "privacy-first digital experiences" speak to a brand position rather than to the job these buyers are accountable for. Nothing about the product needs to change to fix this.

Fit scores say right category, not sharp targeting

All three audiences landed mid-range: 6.1 out of 10 for compliance, 5.8 for IT security, and 5.7 for SME DPOs. That flat, middling pattern is the signature of messaging that puts you in the right category without making any one buyer feel specifically addressed.

Committee buyers cannot assess implementation risk

19% of compliance officers and 24% of SME DPOs did not convert because operational specifics are missing: how DSAR automation actually works, what it integrates with, what governance controls exist, and how long launch takes. The ROI proof is strong, but it answers a different question than the one blocking the demo.

Two findings in this report cost more because of this set. A promise that reads as category-level rather than role-level gives a buyer no reason to prefer one vendor over another, and the missing operational detail on DSAR automation, integrations, and time to launch is exactly what a buyer comparing four options uses to separate them. Both recommendations below are differentiation work as much as conversion work.

Recommended next steps

Both actions are aimed at converting the demand the page is already generating, rather than finding new demand.

Refocus the headline and the primary call to action

The gap is messaging, not demand. Compliance officers hit 3.5%, yet 26% deferred because the demo request competes with repeated "Learn more" links, and 23% said the "Maximize customer trust" framing felt too broad.

Next step Rewrite the top framing to lead with compliance outcomes: audit readiness, defensible consent evidence, and DSAR cycle-time reduction. Make "Request a Demo" the single repeated call to action, and demote "Learn more" to secondary links.

Prioritize compliance-led traffic to this page

Compliance officers posted the highest message-audience fit at 6.1 out of 10, a measure of how closely the copy matches what that audience cares about, and the top conversion rate at 3.5%, or 35 actions per 1,000.

Next step Route more compliance-focused entry points here, such as links from DSAR and cookie and tracker audit content, then measure whether the same action rate holds at higher volume.

How to read this report

What was tested

The live TrustArc Consent and Consumer Rights Solutions landing page, unchanged, run through a simulated conversion funnel against three buyer audiences at 1,000 views each.

What the numbers mean

Simulated CVR is CTA clicks divided by landing page views. Message-audience fit scores how closely the copy matches what an audience cares about, on a 10 point scale. Reason percentages are shares of each audience.

What it is good for

Directional evidence about which message and audience to back next. It is not a forecast of live conversion rate. Real-person testing is the layer that produces proof.
