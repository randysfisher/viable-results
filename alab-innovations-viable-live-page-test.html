What the page is doing well

Signals worth protecting through any rewrite.

Fundraise-prep language is what converts

41% of conversions among investors traced back to copy that names the actual readiness work, such as building a data room and sharpening the story. The page performs best when it leans into concrete fundraise deliverables, which makes that the obvious candidate for the clearest path on the page.

Fractional leadership fits better than its traffic suggests

Growth-stage companies posted the highest message-audience fit at 6.1 out of 10 despite the lowest click rate at 2.1%. The match is already there ahead of the emphasis, so this is a positioning and traffic opportunity rather than a copy problem.

What is holding it back

Four issues account for most of the simulated non-conversions across the three audiences.

"CURIOUS?" does not say what happens next

The single largest blocker on the page. 34% of investors and 29% of founders and growth-stage visitors who did not convert cited the same issue: the primary call to action is a vague link labeled "CURIOUS?" with no indication of what follows the click. When visitors cannot predict the commitment, whether that is a call, a form, an email, or a timeline, they delay even when the offer fits.

Proof is thin where the stakes are highest

20% of investors, 20% of founders, and 23% of growth-stage non-converters rejected the page for lack of concrete proof. There are no quantified outcomes, case results, or verifiable evidence in the visible copy beyond "15+ years" and "12+ years." Capital formation and fractional leadership are high-trust purchases, so brand-forward and conceptual language raises skepticism instead of settling it. 23% of investors deferred for this reason specifically.

Six service lines, no starting point

23% of founders and 19% of growth-stage non-converters said the offer feels too broad. The page lists launch consulting, fractional leadership, strategy, capital formation, program building, and recovery in sequence. That breadth raises the cost of deciding what to start with, and it fed 13% deferral among growth-stage visitors.

The copy reads founder-first to investors

23% of investors felt the copy was written more for founders and operators than for them. That is the best-converting audience on the page telling you the language is aimed slightly past them, which caps how far the current framing can carry your strongest segment.

Recommended next steps

Both actions are aimed at converting the demand the page is already generating, rather than finding new demand.

Make the call to action explicit and specific

This is a messaging problem, not a demand problem. Investors still reached a 2.6% simulated conversion rate, but 34% of the ones who did not convert stopped because "CURIOUS?" never says what happens next. This is the cheapest change on the list and the one with the clearest read.

Next step Replace the "CURIOUS?" link with a concrete action, for example "Book a 20-min capital readiness call" or "Email us for a data-room checklist," and add one line explaining the next step and the time commitment. Then rerun the same page with only this change so the result is attributable.

Aim traffic at fractional leadership

Growth-stage companies posted the highest message-audience fit at 6.1 out of 10, a measure of how closely the page matches what that audience cares about, even though their click rate was the lowest at 2.1%. The fit is there ahead of the emphasis.

Next step Shift outreach and page emphasis toward the fractional leadership section and make it the first pathway visitors can choose. Update the top navigation or the above-the-fold copy to lead with fractional leadership outcomes, with capital formation and launch consulting as secondary links.

How to read this report

What was tested

The live A:LAB Innovations Consulting Services landing page, unchanged, run through a simulated conversion funnel against three buyer audiences at 1,000 views each on September 1, 2026.

What the numbers mean

Simulated CVR is CTA clicks divided by landing page views. Message-audience fit scores how closely the page matches what an audience cares about, on a 10 point scale. Reason percentages are shares of that audience's non-converters.

What it is good for

Directional evidence about which message and audience to back next. It is not a forecast of live conversion rate. Real-person testing is the layer that produces proof.
