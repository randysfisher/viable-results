What the page is doing well

Signals worth protecting through any rewrite.

"First idea to production" is your strongest line

34% of converting Chief AI Officers named the end-to-end delivery promise as their reason for acting. That is the single highest conversion driver in the test. It works because it speaks to the one thing this buyer is accountable for, which is getting past the pilot stage, and it says nothing about which model gets them there.

Governance detail converts rather than reassures

27% of converting Chief AI Officers cited the enterprise rollout specifics: SSO, RBAC, and governance configuration. For a regulated-industry buyer these are not compliance footnotes, they are proof that you have done this before. They are currently positioned as bullet points inside a track description rather than as a headline claim.

What is holding it back

Four issues account for most of the simulated non-conversions. The first appears in every audience, and the second is structural rather than cosmetic.

"Get In Touch" appears in every audience's objections

The most consistent finding in the test: 28% of Chief AI Officers, 26% of VP Engineering, 21% of CIOs, and 21% of CFOs. The page never says what the first conversation is. Buyers could not tell whether they were booking a call, a workshop, or an assessment, how long it takes, or what they receive at the end. Interested executives defer rather than decline.

Claude-first framing narrows the funnel

Vendor specificity was a stated non-conversion driver for 22% of VP Engineering, 21% of Chief AI Officers, and 19% of CIOs. "Starting with Claude," "Building with Claude," and the Anthropic-led framing make the page self-select for buyers who have already picked a model. Executives still choosing between approaches read it as a product page rather than a transformation partner, and leave.

The CFO reads this as a technical document

34% of CFO non-converters said the page reads like technical enablement rather than finance outcomes. Nothing on the page is denominated in cost, return, or risk. In an enterprise AI engagement the CFO is rarely the champion, but they sign, and right now the page gives them nothing to sign for.

CIOs are lost above the fold

The CIO had the lowest rate in the test despite a reasonable 5.3 out of 10 fit score, and 26% called the opening message ambiguous, pointing at "built different built to deliver" and at empty heading artifacts on the page. With bounce around 21%, the drop-off happens before anyone reaches the lower-page proof: the 250+ senior architects, the Fortune 500 track record, and the platform detail.

Table stakes you are missing

A named first step

Every competitor offers at least a partial entry offer. You are the only firm asking an executive to "Get In Touch" without saying what happens next, which is why that objection shows up in all four audiences.

Your edge, underused

Governance proof

Only Deloitte and Ode match your governance detail, and both are bigger, slower engagements to buy. Today it sits in bullet points. It should be a headline claim.

The one to watch

Ode with Anthropic

As long as the page leads with Claude, you are shortlisted next to Anthropic's own team for the same buyer. Outcome-first framing takes you out of that head-to-head.

Recommended next steps

Both actions are aimed at converting the demand the page is already generating, rather than finding new demand.

Lead with production and governance, then name Claude

Chief AI Officers scored the highest message-audience fit at 6.2 out of 10, a measure of how closely the copy matches what an audience cares about, driven by the idea-to-production promise at 34% and rollout governance at 27%. The same audience raised vendor specificity as an objection at 21%. Both things are true at once, and the fix is sequence rather than substance.

Next step Rewrite the opening to lead with production rollout and governance outcomes, then introduce Claude as one implementation path rather than as the premise. Keep every Claude detail on the page. Move it below the outcome.

Replace "Get In Touch" with a defined first step

This is the most consistent finding in the test, appearing in all four audiences at 21% to 28%. The fix is not more capability copy. It is telling an executive exactly what the first meeting is and what they walk away with.

Next step Replace the button and the copy beside it with a named, time-boxed entry offer using your own language, such as a four to eight week Scaling Adoption validation, stating the explicit outputs and what happens after submission. While you are in there, fix the empty heading artifacts that 26% of CIOs noticed above the fold.

How to read this report

What was tested

The live Applied Anthropic Practice landing page, unchanged, run through a simulated conversion funnel against four enterprise buyer audiences at 1,000 views each.

What the numbers mean

Simulated CVR is CTA clicks divided by landing page views, shown with a 95% interval. Message-audience fit scores how closely the copy matches what an audience cares about, on a 10 point scale. Reason percentages are shares of each audience's non-converters.

What it is good for

Directional evidence about which message and audience to back next. It is not a forecast of live conversion rate. Real-person testing is the layer that produces proof.
